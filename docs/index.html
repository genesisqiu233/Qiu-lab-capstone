Introduction

Adaptive decision-making under uncertainty requires organisms to integrate reward magnitude with reward probability and continuously update action values based on experienced outcomes. In rodent probabilistic discounting (PD) tasks, animals choose between a small, certain reward (1 sucrose pellet delivered with 100% probability) and a large, risky reward (4 sucrose pellets delivered with probabilities varying systematically across trial blocks from 100% down to 6.25%). Phasic activity of dopamine neurons in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) encodes reward prediction errors (RPEs)—bursting when outcomes exceed expectations and pausing when expected rewards are omitted—to guide value-based learning and risk preference.

However, because traditional pharmacological lesions or systemic drugs alter neural signaling across entire test sessions, it remains critical to dissociate when during a decision cycle VTA activity causally shapes risk preference. Specifically, does VTA activity primarily govern choice deliberation prior to lever selection, or does it exert its causal influence during outcome evaluation (when small/certain rewards, large/risky wins, or risky reward omissions are experienced)? Answering this question clarifies the neurocomputational mechanisms underlying maladaptive risk-seeking and impaired loss sensitivity in substance use and impulse-control disorders.

Using an approved preclinical optogenetic dataset (VTA_DATA_SQUAD1_FINAL.xlsx), this study investigates how temporally discrete, 532-nm laser inhibition of VTA neurons in Archaerhodopsin-expressing rats (Arch) versus fluorophore control rats (EYFP) alters probabilistic choice across six distinct task epochs (Choice, Certain, CertainITI, RiskyWin, RiskyWinITI, and RiskyLoss). We test three primary hypotheses:

  1. Epoch Specificity Over Global Inhibition: Because VTA activity carries opposite-signed RPE signals during certain versus risky outcomes, laser silencing will not produce a uniform shift across all epochs, but will instead depend on a three-way interaction among viral group (Arch vs. EYFP), laser condition (20mW vs. blocked), and task epoch (TaskPhase).
  2. Bidirectional Outcome-Locked Modulation: In Arch rats, silencing VTA during receipt of the small/certain reward (Certain) will blunt the subjective value of the safe option and increase preference for the large/risky lever, whereas silencing VTA during receipt of a large/risky payoff (RiskyWin) will blunt positive reinforcement of the risky option, decreasing risky choice and increasing lose-shift switching (LSRatio).
  3. Control & Temporal Specificity: Neither EYFP control rats nor Arch rats stimulated during inter-trial interval control windows (CertainITI, RiskyWinITI) will exhibit reliable shifts from baseline choice behavior.

Methods

Data Source, Ethics, and Reproducibility Governance

The dataset (VTA_DATA_SQUAD1_FINAL.xlsx, Squad 1) comprises de-identified behavioral operant logs exported from MED-PC workstations (N = 612 sessions across n = 20 rats). Data access and use were approved for Psychology 9401. All subject identifiers are anonymized alphanumeric codes (DVR02–DVR26), and raw session-level data files are excluded from public version-control tracking (.gitignore) in compliance with laboratory data-sharing governance. The unit of observation is an individual behavioral test session (120 active trials per session across five 24-trial probability blocks: 8 forced-choice and 16 free-choice trials per block).

Subjects, Apparatus, and Experimental Design

Twenty rats received bilateral viral infusions into the VTA of either inhibitory Archaerhodopsin (Arch, n = 13 rats, 426 sessions) or a control fluorophore (EYFP, n = 7 rats, 186 sessions) paired with chronic indwelling optic fibers. During each session, rats chose between a Small/Certain lever (1 pellet, 100% odds) and a Large/Risky lever (4 pellets) across five consecutive probability blocks presented in either Descending (100% → 50% → 25% → 12.5% → 6.25%) or Ascending (6.25% → 12.5% → 25% → 50% → 100%) order.

Across testing phases, intracranial laser delivery (20mW, n = 204 sessions) was interleaved with no-laser baseline sessions (blocked, n = 408 sessions) and time-locked to one of six task epochs encoded in the MED-PC program string (MSN):

  • Choice: Laser stimulation during pre-choice deliberation/lever extension.
  • Certain / CertainITI: Laser stimulation during delivery of the 1-pellet certain reward, or during the corresponding post-reward inter-trial interval (ITI) temporal control window.
  • RiskyWin / RiskyWinITI: Laser stimulation during delivery of the 4-pellet risky reward, or during the corresponding post-win ITI temporal control window.
  • RiskyLoss: Laser stimulation during the unrewarded timeout following an unsuccessful risky lever press.

Data Preparation and Missing-Data Audit

Automated preprocessing in R performed four auditable steps: (1) trimming trailing whitespace from raw column headers (e.g., "Subject "); (2) removing two empty spreadsheet divider columns (Stats by Block (free choice only) and Trial level data); (3) standardizing a single lowercase label ("20mw" \(\to\) "20mW"); and (4) parsing schedule Order (Asc vs. Desc), RiskyLever (L vs. R), and TaskPhase from the MSN identifier. Trial-accounting verification confirmed zero missing values across all session variables and exact trial conservation (\(\text{HR-press} + \text{LR-press} + \text{Omit\_AllTrials} = 120\) active trials in 100% of the 612 sessions).

Statistical Modeling Strategy and Candidate Models

Because sessions are nested within rats (6 to 36 sessions per subject), ordinary least squares regression would violate independence assumptions and underestimate standard errors. We therefore specified a nested hierarchy of linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) with subject-level random intercepts (1 | Subject) predicting the session-level proportion of high-risk free choices (\(\text{Prop\_HR} = \text{HR-press} / [\text{HR-press} + \text{LR-press}]\)):

  • M0 (Null Random-Intercept Model): \(\text{Prop\_HR}_{ij} = \beta_0 + u_{0j} + \epsilon_{ij}\), used to partition variance and compute the intraclass correlation coefficient (\(\text{ICC}\)).
  • M1 (Additive Main-Effects Model): Adds main effects of Group, Condition, TaskPhase, and schedule Order to evaluate average differences without cross-factor moderation.
  • M2 (Global Group \(\times\) Laser Interaction Model): Adds Group * Condition to test whether VTA laser silencing shifts risky choice uniformly across all task phases.
  • M3 (Epoch-Specific Three-Way Interaction Model): Specifies Group * Condition * TaskPhase + Order + (1 | Subject), directly testing whether VTA inhibition alters risky choice in an outcome- and epoch-specific manner.

Candidate models were compared under Maximum Likelihood (REML = FALSE) via Akaike Information Criterion (\(\text{AIC}\)), Bayesian Information Criterion (\(\text{BIC}\)), nested likelihood-ratio tests (\(\chi^2\) LRT), and Nakagawa–Schielzeth marginal (\(R^2_m\)) and conditional (\(R^2_c\)) variance explained. The selected model (M3) was refit via Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML = TRUE) to obtain unbiased variance components and planned within-epoch laser contrasts (20mW − blocked) using emmeans. To verify robustness against bounded proportions, we also fit a sensitivity Binomial generalized linear mixed-effects model (glmer) on discrete lever counts (cbind(HR-press, LR-press)), and fit an analogous LMM on Lose-Shift Ratio (LSRatio) to evaluate trial-by-trial error sensitivity.

In the null model (M0), the intraclass correlation coefficient was \(\text{ICC} = 0.421\), indicating that 42.1% of the variance in session-level risky choice reflected stable individual differences across rats and confirming the necessity of hierarchical modeling. Adding additive main effects (M1) significantly improved fit over M0 (\(\chi^2(8) = 22.03, p = 0.0049\)), whereas adding a global two-way Group × Condition interaction (M2) yielded virtually zero improvement (\(\chi^2(1) = 0.01, p = 0.9125\); \(\text{AIC}\) worsened from -1299.11 to -1297.12). Crucially, allowing the optogenetic effect to vary across task epochs in the three-way interaction model (M3) produced a decisive improvement in model fit (\(\chi^2(15) = 40.62, p = 0.0004\)), achieving the lowest \(\text{AIC}\) (-1307.74) and accounting for 49.6% of total behavioral variance (\(R^2_m = 0.102, R^2_c = 0.496\)). We therefore selected M3 for inferential reporting.

Diagnostic evaluation of M3 confirmed that linear mixed-model assumptions were well satisfied:

  • Residual & Random-Effect Normality: Level-1 residuals (n = 612, SD = 0.074) showed no significant departure from normality (Shapiro–Wilk \(W = 0.995\), \(p = 0.065\)), and Level-2 subject random intercepts (n = 20) were likewise normally distributed (\(W = 0.929\), \(p = 0.147\)).
  • Homoscedasticity & Boundary Behavior: The correlation between absolute residuals and fitted values was small and non-significant (\(r = 0.074\), \(p = 0.066\)). All 612 fitted probabilities lay strictly inside \([0.407, 0.770]\), well away from the \([0, 1]\) boundaries.
  • Binomial GLMM Sensitivity Check: Refitting M3 as a binomial logistic GLMM on discrete choice counts (cbind(HR-press, LR-press)) confirmed the exact same bidirectional pattern on the log-odds scale, demonstrating that inferences are invariant to link specification.

Discussion

Principal Findings and Mechanistic Interpretation

These results demonstrate that VTA neural activity shapes probabilistic risk preference specifically during reward outcome evaluation rather than pre-choice deliberation. Silencing VTA neurons during Certain reward receipt shifted rats toward greater risk-taking (+6.74 pp) and reduced sensitivity to subsequent losses (-8.43 pp lose-shift), consistent with blunting the positive RPE and cached subjective value of the safe 1-pellet alternative. Conversely, silencing VTA neurons during RiskyWin delivery shifted rats away from the risky lever (-4.52 pp) and sharply elevated lose-shift switching (+14.56 pp), consistent with attenuating the positive reinforcement signal needed to sustain risky responding across probabilistic omissions. The absence of effects during CertainITI, RiskyWinITI, and across all epochs in EYFP controls rules out nonspecific light-induced heating, visual distraction, or baseline drift.

Scope of Inference, Limitations, and Implications

Several limitations bound these conclusions. First, the sample contained more Arch (n = 13) than EYFP (n = 7) animals, resulting in wider confidence intervals for EYFP contrasts (SE ≈ 2.7–3.3 pp vs. 1.9–2.1 pp in Arch), though all EYFP point estimates remained near zero. Second, because pan-neuronal VTA Archaerhodopsin inhibits both dopaminergic and local GABAergic neurons, these behavioral data establish VTA circuit necessity during reward receipt without isolating cell-type-specific contributions. Third, session-level linear mixed models summarize choice proportions across 80 free-choice trials; future work can fit trial-by-trial Rescorla–Wagner reinforcement-learning models to the raw trial sequence (Response_1–Response_120) to estimate separate learning-rate parameters (\(\alpha^+\), \(\alpha^-\)) under laser inhibition.

Substantively, identifying a bidirectional, outcome-locked VTA mechanism clarifies how disrupted reward-valuation signals distort risk sensitivity: blunted neural valuation of modest, reliable rewards biases behavior toward risky alternatives, whereas blunted valuation of high-magnitude probabilistic wins induces premature loss-driven switching.

## Introduction

Adaptive decision-making under uncertainty requires organisms to integrate reward magnitude with reward probability and continuously update action values based on experienced outcomes. In rodent probabilistic discounting (PD) tasks, animals choose between a small, certain reward (1 sucrose pellet delivered with 100% probability) and a large, risky reward (4 sucrose pellets delivered with probabilities varying systematically across trial blocks from 100% down to 6.25%). Phasic activity of dopamine neurons in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) encodes reward prediction errors (RPEs)—bursting when outcomes exceed expectations and pausing when expected rewards are omitted—to guide value-based learning and risk preference.

However, because traditional pharmacological lesions or systemic drugs alter neural signaling across entire test sessions, it remains critical to dissociate *when* during a decision cycle VTA activity causally shapes risk preference. Specifically, does VTA activity primarily govern **choice deliberation** prior to lever selection, or does it exert its causal influence during **outcome evaluation** (when small/certain rewards, large/risky wins, or risky reward omissions are experienced)? Answering this question clarifies the neurocomputational mechanisms underlying maladaptive risk-seeking and impaired loss sensitivity in substance use and impulse-control disorders.

Using an approved preclinical optogenetic dataset (`VTA_DATA_SQUAD1_FINAL.xlsx`), this study investigates how temporally discrete, 532-nm laser inhibition of VTA neurons in Archaerhodopsin-expressing rats (`Arch`) versus fluorophore control rats (`EYFP`) alters probabilistic choice across six distinct task epochs (`Choice`, `Certain`, `CertainITI`, `RiskyWin`, `RiskyWinITI`, and `RiskyLoss`). We test three primary hypotheses:

1. **Epoch Specificity Over Global Inhibition:** Because VTA activity carries opposite-signed RPE signals during certain versus risky outcomes, laser silencing will not produce a uniform shift across all epochs, but will instead depend on a three-way interaction among viral group (`Arch` vs. `EYFP`), laser condition (`20mW` vs. `blocked`), and task epoch (`TaskPhase`).
2. **Bidirectional Outcome-Locked Modulation:** In `Arch` rats, silencing VTA during receipt of the small/certain reward (`Certain`) will blunt the subjective value of the safe option and *increase* preference for the large/risky lever, whereas silencing VTA during receipt of a large/risky payoff (`RiskyWin`) will blunt positive reinforcement of the risky option, *decreasing* risky choice and *increasing* lose-shift switching (`LSRatio`).
3. **Control & Temporal Specificity:** Neither `EYFP` control rats nor `Arch` rats stimulated during inter-trial interval control windows (`CertainITI`, `RiskyWinITI`) will exhibit reliable shifts from baseline choice behavior.

## Methods

### Data Source, Ethics, and Reproducibility Governance

The dataset (`VTA_DATA_SQUAD1_FINAL.xlsx`, Squad 1) comprises de-identified behavioral operant logs exported from MED-PC workstations (`N = `r n_sessions`` sessions across `n = `r n_subjects`` rats). Data access and use were approved for Psychology 9401. All subject identifiers are anonymized alphanumeric codes (`DVR02`–`DVR26`), and raw session-level data files are excluded from public version-control tracking (`.gitignore`) in compliance with laboratory data-sharing governance. The unit of observation is an individual behavioral test session (`120` active trials per session across five `24`-trial probability blocks: `8` forced-choice and `16` free-choice trials per block).

### Subjects, Apparatus, and Experimental Design

Twenty rats received bilateral viral infusions into the VTA of either inhibitory Archaerhodopsin (`Arch`, `n = `r n_arch_rats`` rats, `426` sessions) or a control fluorophore (`EYFP`, `n = `r n_eyfp_rats`` rats, `186` sessions) paired with chronic indwelling optic fibers. During each session, rats chose between a Small/Certain lever (1 pellet, 100% odds) and a Large/Risky lever (4 pellets) across five consecutive probability blocks presented in either **Descending** (`100% → 50% → 25% → 12.5% → 6.25%`) or **Ascending** (`6.25% → 12.5% → 25% → 50% → 100%`) order.

Across testing phases, intracranial laser delivery (`20mW`, `n = `r n_laser`` sessions) was interleaved with no-laser baseline sessions (`blocked`, `n = `r n_blocked`` sessions) and time-locked to one of six task epochs encoded in the MED-PC program string (`MSN`):

* **`Choice`**: Laser stimulation during pre-choice deliberation/lever extension.
* **`Certain` / `CertainITI`**: Laser stimulation during delivery of the 1-pellet certain reward, or during the corresponding post-reward inter-trial interval (ITI) temporal control window.
* **`RiskyWin` / `RiskyWinITI`**: Laser stimulation during delivery of the 4-pellet risky reward, or during the corresponding post-win ITI temporal control window.
* **`RiskyLoss`**: Laser stimulation during the unrewarded timeout following an unsuccessful risky lever press.

### Data Preparation and Missing-Data Audit

Automated preprocessing in R performed four auditable steps: (1) trimming trailing whitespace from raw column headers (e.g., `"Subject "`); (2) removing two empty spreadsheet divider columns (`Stats by Block (free choice only)` and `Trial level data`); (3) standardizing a single lowercase label (`"20mw"` $\to$ `"20mW"`); and (4) parsing schedule `Order` (`Asc` vs. `Desc`), `RiskyLever` (`L` vs. `R`), and `TaskPhase` from the `MSN` identifier. Trial-accounting verification confirmed zero missing values across all session variables and exact trial conservation ($\text{HR-press} + \text{LR-press} + \text{Omit\_AllTrials} = 120$ active trials in `100%` of the `612` sessions).

### Statistical Modeling Strategy and Candidate Models

Because sessions are nested within rats (`6` to `36` sessions per subject), ordinary least squares regression would violate independence assumptions and underestimate standard errors. We therefore specified a nested hierarchy of linear mixed-effects models (LMMs) with subject-level random intercepts `(1 | Subject)` predicting the session-level proportion of high-risk free choices ($\text{Prop\_HR} = \text{HR-press} / [\text{HR-press} + \text{LR-press}]$):

* **`M0` (Null Random-Intercept Model):** $\text{Prop\_HR}_{ij} = \beta_0 + u_{0j} + \epsilon_{ij}$, used to partition variance and compute the intraclass correlation coefficient ($\text{ICC}$).
* **`M1` (Additive Main-Effects Model):** Adds main effects of `Group`, `Condition`, `TaskPhase`, and schedule `Order` to evaluate average differences without cross-factor moderation.
* **`M2` (Global Group $\times$ Laser Interaction Model):** Adds `Group * Condition` to test whether VTA laser silencing shifts risky choice uniformly across all task phases.
* **`M3` (Epoch-Specific Three-Way Interaction Model):** Specifies `Group * Condition * TaskPhase + Order + (1 | Subject)`, directly testing whether VTA inhibition alters risky choice in an outcome- and epoch-specific manner.

Candidate models were compared under Maximum Likelihood (`REML = FALSE`) via Akaike Information Criterion ($\text{AIC}$), Bayesian Information Criterion ($\text{BIC}$), nested likelihood-ratio tests ($\chi^2$ LRT), and Nakagawa–Schielzeth marginal ($R^2_m$) and conditional ($R^2_c$) variance explained. The selected model (`M3`) was refit via Restricted Maximum Likelihood (`REML = TRUE`) to obtain unbiased variance components and planned within-epoch laser contrasts (`20mW − blocked`) using `emmeans`. To verify robustness against bounded proportions, we also fit a sensitivity Binomial generalized linear mixed-effects model (`glmer`) on discrete lever counts (`cbind(HR-press, LR-press)`), and fit an analogous LMM on Lose-Shift Ratio (`LSRatio`) to evaluate trial-by-trial error sensitivity.

As detailed in Table 4 and Figure 1, planned simple-effect contrasts from `M3` reveal a double dissociation in `Arch` rats alongside complete null effects in `EYFP` controls:

* **Certain Reward Silencing (`Arch | Certain`):** Inhibiting VTA neurons during delivery of the 1-pellet certain reward significantly **increased** risky lever preference by **`+6.74` percentage points** (`95% CI [+3.11, +10.36] pp`, $t = +3.64, p = 0.0003$), accompanied by an **`-8.43` percentage-point reduction** in lose-shift switching (`95% CI [-15.61, -1.25] pp`, $p = 0.0214$).
* **Risky Win Silencing (`Arch | RiskyWin`):** Conversely, inhibiting VTA neurons during receipt of the 4-pellet risky reward significantly **decreased** risky lever preference by **`-4.52` percentage points** (`95% CI [-8.29, -0.75] pp`, $t = -2.35, p = 0.0189$), driven by a **`+14.56` percentage-point surge** in lose-shift switching (`95% CI [+7.09, +22.03] pp`, $p = 0.0001$).

In `EYFP` control rats, laser delivery produced no significant effect in any of the six epochs (all $|t| \le 1.45$, all $p \ge 0.1481$).

## Discussion

### Principal Findings and Mechanistic Interpretation

These results demonstrate that VTA neural activity shapes probabilistic risk preference specifically during **reward outcome evaluation** rather than pre-choice deliberation. Silencing VTA neurons during `Certain` reward receipt shifted rats toward greater risk-taking (`+6.74 pp`) and reduced sensitivity to subsequent losses (`-8.43 pp` lose-shift), consistent with blunting the positive RPE and cached subjective value of the safe 1-pellet alternative. Conversely, silencing VTA neurons during `RiskyWin` delivery shifted rats away from the risky lever (`-4.52 pp`) and sharply elevated lose-shift switching (`+14.56 pp`), consistent with attenuating the positive reinforcement signal needed to sustain risky responding across probabilistic omissions. The absence of effects during `CertainITI`, `RiskyWinITI`, and across all epochs in `EYFP` controls rules out nonspecific light-induced heating, visual distraction, or baseline drift.

### Scope of Inference, Limitations, and Implications

Several limitations bound these conclusions. First, the sample contained more `Arch` (`n = 13`) than `EYFP` (`n = 7`) animals, resulting in wider confidence intervals for `EYFP` contrasts (`SE ≈ 2.7–3.3 pp` vs. `1.9–2.1 pp` in `Arch`), though all `EYFP` point estimates remained near zero. Second, because pan-neuronal VTA Archaerhodopsin inhibits both dopaminergic and local GABAergic neurons, these behavioral data establish VTA circuit necessity during reward receipt without isolating cell-type-specific contributions. Third, session-level linear mixed models summarize choice proportions across 80 free-choice trials; future work can fit trial-by-trial Rescorla–Wagner reinforcement-learning models to the raw trial sequence (`Response_1`–`Response_120`) to estimate separate learning-rate parameters ($\alpha^+$, $\alpha^-$) under laser inhibition.

Substantively, identifying a bidirectional, outcome-locked VTA mechanism clarifies how disrupted reward-valuation signals distort risk sensitivity: blunted neural valuation of modest, reliable rewards biases behavior toward risky alternatives, whereas blunted valuation of high-magnitude probabilistic wins induces premature loss-driven switching.
